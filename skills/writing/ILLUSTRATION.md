# Formula's illustration style — the brief an agent writes for image generation

Every Formula piece that needs artwork gets a generated illustration, and the
agent writes the brief. The style below is fixed and does not change per post;
only the scene does. Paste the style block verbatim, then your scene paragraph.

Audience is 45–60 and non-technical. The picture makes one idea legible at
thumbnail size. It never decorates.

## The style block — copy exactly

```
Flat vector illustration in a strict two-colour system: solid black and
orange on a pure white background. No other colours.

Style rules, followed exactly:
- Clothing and body masses are solid black silhouettes with no internal
  shading or outline.
- Hair is a single solid orange blob shape. Skin is left white with a thin
  black contour; faces are minimal — a small dot eye, a simple nose line,
  no mouth detail. Hands are white with thin black outlines.
- Screens, panels, boxes and objects are drawn as thin uniform black
  outlines on white, with body text suggested by evenly spaced thin
  horizontal rules rather than real letters.
- Exactly one element carries a smooth orange-to-yellow gradient fill; the
  rest of the orange is flat.
- Consistent thin stroke weight throughout. No gradients on black shapes,
  no shadows, no texture, no 3D, no perspective depth.

Wide 16:9 composition, generous white space, everything grounded on an
invisible baseline.

No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos anywhere in the image.
```

## The scene paragraph — you write this per post

One paragraph, three or four sentences, naming what is in frame and where. Rules:

- **Draw the argument, not the subject.** A post about paying for packaging is
  two identical machines, one gift-wrapped — not a picture of an AI.
- **Two objects or one person and one object.** More than three elements is mush
  at thumbnail size.
- **Say which element carries the gradient.** It is the thing the piece is
  sceptical of, or the thing the reader wants; pick one and say so.
- **Give posture a job.** Reaching, turning away, not noticing. Posture is how a
  flat illustration argues.
- No metaphors that need the article to decode. No robots, brains, circuit
  boards, glowing orbs, rocket ships, lightbulbs, chess pieces or handshakes.

## Sizes

| Where | Size |
|---|---|
| Medium featured image | 1500 × 844 |
| Ghost post feature image | 1200 × 675 |

Medium crops the preview card, so keep the subject centred with margin.
